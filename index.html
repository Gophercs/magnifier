The tools

ZOOM

Zoom dialAndroid

A geared thumb roller: slow drags make tiny changes, quick flicks go a long way. Press and drag straight from the button. Uses the camera's own zoom, up to 30× on some phones (digital zoom on iPhone). Pinch works too.

MF

Fine manual focusAndroid

Press AF and drag to focus by hand in tiny steps, shown to a hundredth of a centimetre. Or twist two fingers on the picture like a lens ring.

Tap, hold and lock focusAndroid

Tap to focus on a spot. Hold, and it finds the sharpest focus for that spot by itself, then locks it so you can fine-tune with the dial.

LightAndroid

Switch on the phone's torch for dim restaurants and dark cupboards. It switches off while a picture is frozen and comes back after.

Freeze

Freeze the picture, then pinch and pan around it at leisure: handy when the phone is poked into a gap at an awkward angle. In manual focus, a big circle in the middle of the screen works as the button.

Focus highlighting

Paints the crisp edges pink so you can see exactly what's in focus (often called focus peaking).

Focus stackingAndroid

Takes five pictures through a few centimetres of focus, lines them up and combines the sharpest parts, for close-ups that are sharp from front to back. You can also pick any single frame.

Your shots

Every freeze is kept in a gallery on your phone, with pinch and dial zoom for a closer look, and sharing.

Steady hands built in

The dials ignore accidental brushes, and a lift-off filter stops the setting slipping as your thumb comes off.

Android marks tools that need Android's camera controls. iPhones don't give web pages control of the light, focus or optical zoom.

A magnifying glass without the ads

Most magnifier apps are free in name only: they make you sit through adverts before you can read the label you're squinting at, or nag you to subscribe. Proper Useful Magnifier has none of that. It's a small web app with no account, no tracking and no adverts. Open it in your browser, point your camera and read. It's useful as a reading magnifier for small print, a low-vision aid, a loupe for checking electronics, stamps, coins, fabrics and splinters, or a close-up camera with a light for anything hard to see.

Because it uses the phone's real camera controls rather than just blowing up pixels, it gets much more out of the lens than a basic zoom: hardware zoom, the closest focus the lens can manage, and focus you can set by hand.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no sign-up, no subscription, no in-app purchases.

Does it work on iPhone?

Partly. In Safari the magnifier, digital zoom, freeze, gallery and focus highlighting work. Apple doesn't let web pages control the iPhone camera's light, focus or optical zoom, so those tools only appear on Android. Every browser on an iPhone uses Safari's engine, so Chrome on iPhone is no different.

Are my pictures uploaded?

No. The camera picture is processed on your phone and never sent anywhere. Shots you keep are stored in your browser on the phone; clearing the site's data removes them.

Which browser should I use?

Chrome on Android gives the full set of controls. Firefox on Android shows the camera but doesn't let pages control zoom, light or focus.

Why can't I choose the macro or wide lens?

Many phones only offer web pages one back camera and pick the lens themselves. If your phone lists more, you can choose between them in settings.
